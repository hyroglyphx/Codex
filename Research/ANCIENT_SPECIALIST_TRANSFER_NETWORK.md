# Ancient Specialist-Transfer and Translation-Junction Network

## Status

**Research object:** candidate network architecture  
**Method:** TCIM / artifact-first / provenance-first  
**Scope:** eastern Mediterranean, Egypt, Anatolia, Black Sea, Colchis, and selected western extensions  
**Primary caution:** network connectivity is not evidence of common origin. Every edge must retain its own provenance and relation type.

## Research question

When populations, specialists, ritual personnel, interpreters, craftspeople, soldiers, merchants, and political information move between ancient centers, which operational structures survive translation into new languages, institutions, sanctuaries, and political systems?

This study replaces a one-directional "influence" model with a typed transport graph.

```text
PERSON / POPULATION / OBJECT / PROCEDURE / INFORMATION
                    |
                    v
              TRANSPORT EDGE
                    |
                    v
        LOCAL RE-ENCODING / INSTITUTION
                    |
                    v
        SURVIVING OPERATIONAL INVARIANTS
```

## Epistemic firewall

The existing Codex integrity rules apply without exception:

```text
raw witness != observation != reading != interpretation != historical claim
similarity != transmission
translation equivalence != etymology
network path != demonstrated historical transfer
mythic itinerary != demographic event
```

A relation may be **ATTESTED**, **OBSERVED**, **DERIVED**, **CANDIDATE**, **BOUNDED**, or **REJECTED**. Candidate transmission requires evidence beyond resemblance.

## Transport operators

### Population fission

```text
P -> P1 + P2
```

A civic or ethnic population divides, with one branch remaining or returning and another continuing to a new settlement. Herodotus' account of the Phocaean evacuation and partial return is a primary test case.

### Specialist relocation

```text
S(A) -> S(B)
```

A state, court, temple, army, or migration moves trained specialists. Mesopotamian deportation and concentration of skilled populations supplies a comparative mechanism; Egyptian settlement of Ionian and Carian military communities and the resulting interpreter tradition supplies a Herodotean comparison.

### Specialist deployment / daughter institution

```text
K(S) -> N
```

Operational knowledge carried by a specialist, priestly family, interpreter, craft group, sacred object, or institutional packet establishes a new node.

### Expeditionary remnant

```text
A -> A'
```

An expeditionary population is remembered as remaining abroad and becoming a local population. Herodotus' Egyptian genealogy for the Colchians is recorded as an ancient population-transfer claim, not promoted here as demonstrated demographic history.

### Cultural parentage / ancestral incorporation

A successor institution embeds an older authority, object, procedure, or specialist inside a new local construction. The carrier may become locally renamed while procedural invariants persist.

## Junction classes

Three recurring node classes organize the graph:

```text
PORT <-> COURT <-> ORACLE / SANCTUARY
```

Some locations combine several classes and therefore deserve higher testing priority.

### Naucratis

Candidate functions:
- regulated port and market interface
- multi-community sanctuary environment
- Egyptian / Greek / Cypriot / Phoenician contact
- Ionian and Carian personnel ecology
- translation and interpreter infrastructure

Herodotus 2.178 identifies Phocaea, Chios, Teos, and Clazomenae among the Ionian cities participating in the Hellenion, while Miletus, Samos, and Aegina maintained distinct sanctuaries.

### Western Anatolia: Miletus / Lydia / Caria-Lycia

Candidate functions:
- east-west court diplomacy
- maritime redistribution
- military and mercenary mobility
- astronomical and calendrical knowledge
- oracle consultation and information routing
- Carian-Ionian contact with Egypt
- access to southwest Anatolian divinatory traditions

The unit of analysis must not be "Greek influence." Miletus, Chios, Erythrae, Phocaea, Lydia, Caria, and neighboring communities retain distinct language, alliance, political, and institutional edges.

### Telmessian interpretive node

Herodotus 1.78 presents Telmessian interpreters decoding an anomalous event involving horses and serpents. The testable object is the **operator grammar**:

```text
phenomenon
 -> classification
 -> symbolic assignment
 -> relation-preserving interpretation
 -> political prediction
```

Research question: does this procedure share independently constrained operational features with Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Anatolian, or other eastern Mediterranean omen systems?

Do not promote an Egypt -> Telmessos transmission edge without independent historical and procedural evidence.

### Delphi and other oracle nodes

Oracle centers may act as information-routing and decision nodes as well as ritual centers. Relevant cases include:
- Lydian consultation
- migration and settlement decisions
- ritual remediation after anomalous events
- elite guest-friendship carrying oracle information between courts/cities

### Phocaea

Phocaea is a high-value mobility node because several independent edge types converge:
- northern Ionian speech grouping
- participation at Naucratis
- extreme western navigation
- relationship with Tartessus
- Persian-pressure evacuation
- population fission / partial return
- oracle-directed western relocation
- interaction with Etruscan and Carthaginian powers

This is a trajectory, not a stationary ethnonym.

### Colchis / Phasis / Caucasian interface

Colchis should be modeled as an eastern interface rather than an exotic endpoint.

Relevant layers must remain separate:
1. Herodotus' legendary Argonaut/Medea framing.
2. Herodotus' Egyptian-Colchian population claim.
3. historical Black Sea maritime networks.
4. Milesian / Ionian colonial and commercial traditions.
5. later evidence for multilingual Caucasian-facing market nodes.
6. technical-resource and specialist traditions.

The primary corridor hypothesis is:

```text
Egypt / Nile
    <->
Naucratis
    <->
Ionia / western Anatolia
    <->
Black Sea
    <->
Colchis / Caucasus
```

with a western extension:

```text
Ionia / Phocaea -> central Mediterranean -> Tartessus / Iberia
```

This is a transport topology to test, not a claim of a single continuous cultural lineage.

## Herodotean alliance and information edges

Store alliance, subjection, friendship, reciprocity, mediation, language affinity, and sanctuary participation as different relation types.

Examples requiring source-addressed records include:
- Miletus <-> Chios: reciprocal military assistance.
- Miletus <-> Lydia: war followed by guest-friendship/alliance under Alyattes.
- Lydia <-> Media: peace settlement mediated by Cilician and Babylonian actors and reinforced by dynastic marriage.
- Croesus / Lydia <-> Sparta: friendship and alliance.
- Croesus / Lydia <-> Egypt and Babylon: external alliance relationships in the Persian-war context.
- Phocaea <-> Erythrae: speech/dialect affinity, not automatically political affinity.
- Phocaea <-> Naucratis/Hellenion: institutional sanctuary participation.
- Phocaea <-> Tartessus: elite friendship/patronage tradition.
- Phocaean migration -> oracle interpretation -> western settlement: decision-routing edge.

## Argonaut / Medea comparative layer

Herodotus opens his Histories with competing Persian/Greek narratives that explicitly place the voyage to Aea/Colchis and the removal of Medea inside a larger east-west reciprocity story.

For Codex purposes, the mythic structure is typed separately from historical transfer:

```text
distant expedition
 + local knowledge-holder
 + difficult acquisition
 -> removal / mobility of knowledge-holder
 -> knowledge travels with person
```

This can be compared structurally with specialist transport without treating myth as direct documentary evidence.

## Promotion tests

A candidate transfer relation should not be promoted merely because two nodes are connected. Prefer convergence across:

1. **Procedural constraint** — same ordered operation, not just same symbol.
2. **Historical contact** — plausible personnel or institutional route.
3. **Chronology** — source and receiving states are temporally admissible.
4. **Independent witnesses** — preferably three independent evidentiary lanes.
5. **Lexical/technical constraint** — where relevant, regular linguistic or technical correspondence.
6. **Institutional carrier** — identifiable court, sanctuary, guild, military settlement, interpreter class, family, school, or port.
7. **Holdout behavior** — model predicts an unexamined relation or feature.
8. **Null comparison** — competing routes and generic convergence are tested.

- Separate ethnic label, language, polity, city, sanctuary, and population state.
- Build a Naucratis community subgraph.
- Build a Miletus two-front subgraph: Egypt-facing and Black-Sea/Colchis-facing.
- Encode Phocaean migration as state transitions rather than a single "colonization" edge.
- Compare Telmessian omen operators against Egyptian and Mesopotamian divination corpora.
- Record Mesopotamian skilled-deportation cases as comparative transport controls.
- Add negative controls where contact exists but procedural invariants do not.
- Preserve failed transmission predictions.

## Core question

> When the people, language, ruler, sanctuary, and deity change, what part of the interpretive or technical procedure survives?
