## Textual setting and procedure

The baptismal renunciation formula brings together **Melchisedekites, Theodotians and Athinganoi**. It includes doctrines concerning Melchizedek alongside accusations of divination and ritual separation. Starr says his translated excerpt omits shorter references to the first two groups. Their shared treatment by the writer must not be converted into established community identity.

The formula alleges this sequence:

1. Invoke named powers.
2. Draw down and question the moon.
3. Assign human names to stars.
4. Set the stars in opposition and associate their relative fortunes with the corresponding people.

This is a **reported procedure in a hostile religious document**, not an independently observed performance. The names are not individually glossed as sun, fire or lightning. The spring-water detail occurs in the companion-tract account discussed by Starr, not in this formula.

Comparative research object: person → named star → celestial interaction → human consequence. Compare ordered operations, target assignment and result interpretation; a generic reference to magic is insufficient for structural homology or transmission.

## Chronology, identity and witness dependence

Starr pp. 99–100 reports a generally accepted ninth-century dating but emphasizes unresolved dating questions. He also discusses an erroneous attribution to Patriarch Methodios associated with a scribal rubric. This record therefore uses **approximately ninth century, scholarly estimate**, not a precise composition date or secure named authorship.

The passage identifies Athinganoi, not a Romani-speaking population through independent linguistic evidence. Preserve two competing hypotheses:

- population continuity between earlier Athinganoi and later Roma;
- transfer of an existing label to a later population.

Purity practices, divination accusations and repeated labels do not alone distinguish these hypotheses. Translations and mirrors are not independent witnesses. Dependence between the formula and companion tract remains unresolved.
