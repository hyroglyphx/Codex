Date: 2026-10-30. Parent: [candidate network](ANCIENT_SPECIALIST_TRANSFER_NETWORK.md).

## Evidence contract

ATTESTED means the relation or claim is present in the cited narrative, not that its historical occurrence is independently established. BOUNDED identifies an explicitly limited inference; REJECTED applies only to the proposition stated. All records below depend on ONE literary witness: Herodotus, Histories, composed in the fifth century BCE. Multiple chapters, reported informants, translations, and web mirrors do not count as independent witnesses.

Provenance for every row: Greek literary narrative accessed through G. C. Macaulay's English translation on Lexundria, checked 2026-10-30. The row's source link resolves the exact book/chapter; its provenance column distinguishes narrative report, attributed testimony, and authorial inference. Readings below are generated paraphrases of recovered translation text; Greek collation and manuscript criticism remain pending. Chronology uses source-relative reigns and event order, deliberately avoiding unsupported exact BCE years.

City, civic community, ruler, sanctuary, specialist group, and migrant cohort are distinct entities. Arrows express the relation specified, not automatic movement of people or knowledge. Migration uses existing PERSONNEL_RELOCATION, POPULATION_FISSION and SETTLEMENT types; no new generic migration type is required.

Mythic itineraries remain excluded from demographic edges. No similarity, ancestry assertion, or composed network path is promoted to demonstrated transmission.

## Single highest-value next witness

**Target: British Museum 1886,0401.509**, an inscribed pottery fragment catalogued from the Apollo sanctuary at Naukratis ([object record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/G_1886-0401-509)).

Why this witness: it introduces a material lane independent of Herodotus and directly tests the Apollo/Naucratis institution in H07. This is a bounded choice for the present slice, not a claim that it resolves the entire corridor.

Acquisition status: catalogue search metadata identifies the findspot and Apollo as named in the inscription; full object inspection, date, image, transcription and excavation context remain pending. Do not assume it names Milesian Apollo or establishes the dedicator's civic identity.

Next-pass protocol: obtain object image and published reading; preserve visible letters versus restoration; record dating basis and findspot reliability; determine whether Apollo alone, a Milesian epithet, or a donor ethnic is actually present. Apollo alone supports the local cult, not Milesian management; a securely read Milesian epithet would constrain the institutional link, still without proving procedural transfer. An unreadable inscription or insecure provenance remains a preserved bounded result.
