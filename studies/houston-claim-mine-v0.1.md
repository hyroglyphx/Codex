## Bite 01 — Memnon / western Anatolia / Karabel

### Houston's claim cluster

Houston identifies Amenhotep III with the Homeric Memnon and uses Memnon as part of a broad Ethiopia/Egypt/Asia contact narrative. In the same chapter she extends Egyptian/Ethiopian influence into Asia Minor.

The identity equation itself is not required for the useful result.

### Ancient witness: Herodotus 2.106

Herodotus reports two rock-cut warrior figures in Ionia:
- one on the Ephesus–Phocaea road;
- one on the Sardis–Smyrna road.

He describes the equipment/appearance as "both Egyptian and Ethiopian" and says that some viewers identified the figures as **Memnon**, although Herodotus himself rejected that identification and attributed them to Sesostris.

This preserves a 5th-century-BCE reception fact:

```
WEST ANATOLIAN ROCK RELIEF
-> EGYPTIAN / ETHIOPIAN VISUAL CLASSIFICATION
-> MEMNON IDENTIFICATION BY SOME GREEK VIEWERS
```

### Material control: Karabel

The surviving Karabel relief lies in the western Anatolian pass between Ephesos and Sardis. J. D. Hawkins' 1998 reading identifies its figure as:

```
Tarkasnawa
King of Mira
[son of] a king of Mira
grandson of a king of Mira
```

The monument belongs to the Late Bronze Age Luwian / Mira political environment, not an Egyptian royal inscription.

Karabel is often associated with the Herodotean report, but the road description is imperfect and another similar relief may have been meant. Preserve:

```
HERODOTEAN RELIEF REPORT ~ KARABEL
= HIGH-VALUE IDENTIFICATION CANDIDATE
not exact-object certainty
```
