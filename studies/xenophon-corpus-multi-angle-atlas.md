# Xenophon Corpus — Multi-Angle Research Atlas

Status: ACTIVE
Updated: 2026-09-21

## Principle

Use Xenophon's corpus as a controlled multi-genre experiment.

The authorial environment remains relatively stable while the observational mode changes:

```text
participant
historian
political theorist
Socratic memorialist
dialogue writer
household/economic observer
constitutional ethnographer
encomiast
technical practitioner
policy economist
social dramatist
```

A concept that recurs across several modes deserves more weight than a formulation appearing only in one genre.

A difference across modes is also evidence: genre itself may be the transform.

## Core corpus lanes

### XEN-01 — Anabasis
Mode: participant route narrative / military memoir-history.

Highest value:
- peoples encountered along an actual route,
- interpreters and cross-language contact,
- Persian and Anatolian political geography,
- food, tribute, hospitality, markets, rivers, distances, local customs,
- command under crisis,
- coalition discipline,
- leader-to-contingent shifts,
- real-time adaptation rather than idealized Cyrus narrative.

Primary comparison:
```text
Anabasis practical coalition behavior
↔ Cyropaedia idealized coalition behavior
```

Use as an adversarial control on the Cyrus consolidation operator.

### XEN-02 — Hellenica
Mode: historical narrative.

Value:
- interstate alliances,
- defections,
- diplomacy,
- constitutional change,
- leadership failure/success,
- military and political sequencing,
- same writer under less utopian narrative constraints.

Use to distinguish:
```text
Xenophontic political ideal
from
Xenophontic historical description
```

### XEN-03 — Oeconomicus
Mode: household/agricultural management dialogue.

Value:
- oikos as governance unit,
- household hierarchy,
- estate administration,
- agriculture,
- work allocation,
- education/training,
- gendered household roles,
- management of dependents and enslaved labor.

Research question:
Does the Cyrus operator scale downward?

```text
household order
↔ military order
↔ political order
```

Do not assume identical ethical status across scales; test vocabulary and operator structure.

### XEN-04 — Hiero
Mode: dialogue on tyranny/rulership.

Value:
- ruler psychology,
- fear and security,
- pleasure/desire,
- reward,
- honor,
- loyalty,
- transformation of coercive rule into beneficial leadership.

This is one of the strongest conceptual controls for Cyropaedia.

Compare:
```text
Hiero: explicit argument about ruling
↔ Cyropaedia: narrative demonstration of ruling
```

### XEN-05 — Memorabilia
Mode: Socratic recollection/apologetic philosophy.

Value:
- self-command,
- usefulness/expertise,
- friendship,
- education,
- leadership,
- household and public management,
- Socratic argument structure.

Compare Xenophon's philosophical vocabulary with the political behavior dramatized in Cyropaedia.

### XEN-06 — Symposium
Mode: social-dialogic performance.

Value:
- status display,
- praise and self-description,
- eros/beauty,
- entertainment,
- social bonding,
- joking and competition,
- how identities are negotiated in a group setting.

Useful for:
```text
affective influence
performance
prestige
group cohesion
```

This is a social-scale control for the "goēteia"/captivation dimension observed in Cyropaedia.

### XEN-07 — Apology
Mode: Socratic defense/death narrative.

Value:
Direct comparison with Plato's Apology and other Socratic traditions.

This is an excellent parallel-witness control:
```text
same historical figure/event family
+ same language
+ different authorial construction
```

Useful for studying how narrative purpose changes the representation of one person.

### XEN-08 — Agesilaus
Mode: encomium.

High-value internal control:
Xenophon also narrates Agesilaus in Hellenica.

Therefore:
```text
same author
+ same historical actor
+ different genre
=
GENRE-TRANSFORM CALIBRATION
```

Compare:
- event selection,
- omissions,
- virtues foregrounded,
- speeches,
- causality,
- leader→people compression,
- praise vocabulary.

This is one of the cleanest internal controls in the entire corpus.

### XEN-09 — Constitution of the Lacedaemonians
Mode: institutional/constitutional ethnography.

Value:
- education,
- discipline,
- age/status classes,
- civic institutions,
- military-social organization,
- customs designed to reproduce a political order,
- later degeneration/corruption problem.

Compare:
```text
Lycurgan institutional engineering
↔ Cyrus coalition/institution building
```

### XEN-10 — Ways and Means / Poroi
Mode: economic-policy proposal.

Value:
- state revenue,
- peace versus hegemonic war,
- trade,
- resident foreigners/metics,
- silver-mining economy,
- public economic incentives.

This provides a non-imperial economic angle on:
```text
wealth → stability → political capacity
```

Compare with Cyropaedia's booty/reward/alliance economy.

### XEN-11 — On the Cavalry Commander / Hipparchicus
Mode: technical military handbook.

Value:
- cavalry organization,
- training,
- command,
- reconnaissance,
- morale,
- display,
- deception,
- tactical vocabulary.

Use as a lexical/technical control for cavalry passages in Cyropaedia and Anabasis.

### XEN-12 — On Horsemanship
Mode: technical animal/embodied-practice handbook.

Value:
- horse anatomy/selection,
- training,
- handling,
- rider behavior,
- display and movement,
- technical vocabulary.

Important methodological use:
technical prose gives comparatively low-metaphor controls for words that may become political metaphors elsewhere.

### XEN-13 — On Hunting / Cynegeticus
Mode: hunting/education treatise.

Authorship caution:
modern scholarship has questioned Xenophontic authorship.

Use only with explicit authorship-confidence field.

Potential value:
- pursuit,
- training,
- endurance,
- terrain,
- human/animal cognition,
- education through disciplined practice.

## Same-author genre transform

Define:
```text
G(X,c) = realization of concept c under Xenophontic genre X
```

Compare concepts across works rather than assuming a single "Xenophon doctrine."

Candidate dimensions:
- leadership,
- persuasion,
- obedience,
- reward,
- honor,
- trust,
- discipline,
- household/state ordering,
- wealth,
- display,
- friendship/alliance,
- education,
- self-control,
- technical expertise.

For each concept:
1. exact Greek lemma(s);
2. work and passage;
3. speaker/narrator;
4. genre;
5. social scale;
6. action sequence;
7. translation history;
8. translator overlays;
9. cross-work convergence;
10. contradictions/divergences.

## Translation lattice

For every high-value passage compare:
- Greek critical text,
- Latin translation where available,
- early vernacular translations,
- nineteenth-century English (including Dakyns/Watson where relevant),
- Loeb-era translation,
- modern scholarly translation,
- modern translations in at least two non-English languages where useful.

## Benchmark — XEN-CORPUS-01

Choose one operator:
```text
WIN / MAINTAIN ALLEGIANCE
```

Trace it through:
1. Cyropaedia — Gobryas / Hyrcanians / Armenians;
2. Anabasis — coalition maintenance under danger;
3. Hiero — ruler's incentives and subjects;
4. Oeconomicus — management and willing cooperation;
5. Memorabilia — leadership/benefit arguments;
6. Constitution of Lacedaemonians — institutional discipline;
7. Ways and Means — civic incentives.

This tests whether the Cyrus philosophy is:
- unique to Cyropaedia,
- a recurring Xenophontic operator,
- or a genre-specific realization of a broader management philosophy.

## Benchmark — XEN-CORPUS-02

GENRE TRANSFORM:
```text
Agesilaus in Hellenica
↔ Agesilaus in Agesilaus
```

Measure exactly what praise genre changes while author and subject remain largely controlled.

This should become a calibration anchor for interpreting the historical/philosophical transformation in Cyropaedia.
