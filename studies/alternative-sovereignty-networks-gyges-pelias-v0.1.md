## Macro result

The Gyges-Candaules story and the Jason-Pelias-Medea cycle both portray sovereignty as vulnerable to **parallel power structures** rather than only to direct battlefield conquest.

Canonical operator:

```text
INCUMBENT KING
-> legitimacy fracture
-> alternative institutional nodes activate
-> coalition bypasses royal command channel
-> sovereignty is transferred / destroyed
-> oracle or ritual framework interprets the transfer
```

## 1. Gyges: a palace coup assembled from non-royal nodes

Herodotus 1.8-13 gives:

```text
Candaules = incumbent Heraclid king
Gyges = trusted bodyguard / military-household officer
Queen = dynastic household + access to royal chamber
Lydian armed factions = political coercive constituency
Delphi = supra-local sacral adjudicator
```

The queen supplies the immediate coup design and weapon; Gyges supplies military-household access and execution; the Lydians contest the seizure by arms; Delphi arbitrates the succession and converts a violent transfer into recognized kingship.

## 2. Pelias: the king is already framed as an improper holder

Pindar Pythian 4 has Jason return to Iolcus claiming his father's ancient royal honor, saying Pelias took it by force from its rightful holders. Pelias promises to yield royal power after Jason retrieves the fleece.

Thus the Argonautic expedition begins as a succession dispute displaced into an external impossible task:

```text
RIGHTFUL CLAIMANT
-> EXILE / EXTRA-PALATIAL FORMATION
-> RETURN
-> INCUMBENT DEFLECTS CLAIM INTO QUEST
```

## 3. Jason's alternative network is institutionally diverse

Jason is not raised by the palace but by **Chiron**. He then mobilizes a transregional heroic coalition by heralds. Pindar's expedition includes:

```text
Chiron = educator / extra-palatial wisdom
Jason = claimant / coalition organizer
Argonauts = pan-regional military-heroic network
Orpheus = ritual / song authority
Mopsus = seer / divinatory authority
Hera = divine patron
Argo = technological / logistical platform
```

The claimant therefore approaches Pelias with a support architecture that is not reducible to one city's army.

## 4. Medea is the decisive priestly-technical node

In Apollonius Rhodius, Medea is a priestess of Hecate and an expert in pharmaka. She is also daughter of the Colchian king Aeetes.

Her role combines:

```text
FOREIGN ROYAL STATUS
+ PRIESTLY ACCESS
+ TECHNICAL / RITUAL KNOWLEDGE
+ PALACE PENETRATION
```

She enables Jason to acquire the fleece and, after the return, becomes the instrument by which Pelias is eliminated. In Apollodorus, she enters Pelias' palace and persuades his daughters through a staged rejuvenation demonstration.

Thus Pelias is not removed by a conventional battle for the throne. He is destroyed through:

```text
RITUAL KNOWLEDGE
-> DOMESTIC TRUST
-> DYNASTIC WOMEN
-> INTERNAL PALACE ACTION
```

## 6. Pelias / Pelasgians

`Pelias` (Πελίας) and `Pelasgian/Pelasgos` (Πελασγός / Πελασγοί) should not presently be treated as an established etymological pair.

But the **structural resemblance** is worth retaining separately:

- Pelasgian traditions repeatedly concern older populations, displacement, incorporation, renaming and survival under later political identities.
- Pelias' story concerns a disputed older right, displacement of the legitimate line, return from outside the palace, and eventual overthrow through parallel networks.

## 8. Astyages and the Magi — priestly-divinatory power as a succession node

Herodotus makes the Magi more than neutral dream technicians.

First, at 1.101 the Magi are listed as one of the six Median tribes. In the Astyages cycle, a subset of Magi functions as royal dream interpreters.

Sequence:

```text
Astyages' first dream
-> Magian interpretation
-> dynastic marriage strategy altered
-> Mandane married to Persian Cambyses

second dream
-> same interpretive class
-> predicted grandson will replace Astyages
-> attempted elimination of Cyrus

Cyrus survives and is ritually/socially 'made king' in children's play
-> Astyages recalls same Magi
-> Magi reinterpret prophecy as already discharged
-> Cyrus is released

Cyrus later revolts successfully
-> Astyages impales the Magian interpreters
```

The key political admission occurs in Herodotus 1.120: the Magi explicitly tell Astyages that if Cyrus becomes king, rule will pass from the Medes to a Persian, whereas under Astyages, 'our countryman,' they share in power and receive great honor.

This is direct narrative evidence for:

```text
DIVINATORY AUTHORITY
+ ETHNIC / CORPORATE INTEREST
+ ACCESS TO KING
-> SUCCESSION POLICY
```

### 8.1 Parallel-sovereignty triad in the fall of Astyages

Herodotus' Median collapse can be decomposed into three semi-independent power systems:

```text
ROYAL HOUSE: Astyages / Mandane / Cyrus
PRIESTLY-DIVINATORY NODE: Magi
MILITARY-ELITE NODE: Harpagus + chief Medes
```

The Magi shape the king's succession responses; Harpagus quietly builds the elite-military coalition that finally transfers sovereignty to Cyrus.

Thus:

```text
ROYAL DECISION
depends on
SACRAL INTERPRETATION
while
MILITARY/ELITE NETWORK
can independently reverse the throne
```

This strongly parallels the Gyges and Pelias stories: sovereignty is distributed across institutions surrounding the crown.
