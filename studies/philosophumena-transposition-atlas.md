# Philosophumena / Refutation of All Heresies — Transposition Atlas

Status: ACTIVE SOURCE STUDY
Updated: 2026-09-21

## Research role

Treat the *Refutation of All Heresies* (often called *Philosophumena*) as both:

1. a late antique witness preserving earlier philosophical, ritual, and Christian materials;
2. a polemical machine that actively reclassifies those materials.

The work is therefore not a transparent catalogue.

Its useful transformation is:

```text
earlier source / teacher / rite / doctrine
        ↓
quotation or paraphrase
        ↓
authorial comparison
        ↓
claimed genealogy of dependence
        ↓
heresiological classification
        ↓
later manuscript / translation
```

Each arrow must be stored separately.

## 1. The work itself is stratified

The extant textual state is discontinuous.

- Book I circulated separately and was once attributed to Origen.
- Books II–III are lost.
- The beginning of Book IV is lost.
- Most of IV and Books V–X survive through the manuscript recovered from Mount Athos in the nineteenth century.
- The attribution to Hippolytus remains debated in modern scholarship.

Therefore:

```text
WORK_IDENTITY != EXTANT_TEXT_STATE != AUTHORSHIP_CERTAINTY
```

This is a direct application of the Gilgamesh/Homer/Jubilees lesson.

## 2. Traversal architecture

The work behaves like an intellectual periplus.

### Stage A — source bank
Book I surveys:
- physical/natural philosophers,
- ethical philosophers,
- dialecticians,
- Stoics,
- Epicurus,
- Pyrrho,
- Brahmans,
- Druids,
- Hesiod.

The lost and fragmentary early books extend the preparatory survey into Egyptian, Chaldean, astrological, magical, and ritual knowledge.

### Stage B — heresiological traversal
Books V–IX move through named teachers and schools:
- Naasseni
- Peratae
- Sethians
- Justinus
- Simon
- Valentinus and later Valentinians
- Basilides and followers
- Marcion
- other Christian movements
- Noetus / Callistus
- Elchasai and related contemporary disputes

### Stage C — return / recapitulation
Book X compresses the preceding survey and returns to the author's positive account of truth.

Architecture:

```text
MAP SOURCES
→ WALK THROUGH GROUPS
→ ASSIGN DERIVATIONS
→ RECAPITULATE
→ RETURN TO NORMATIVE CENTER
```

This makes *Philosophumena* structurally comparable to other traversal texts without asserting literary dependence:
- *Argonautica*: place-by-place cultural traversal
- *Cyropaedia*: people-by-people political traversal
- *Philosophumena*: doctrine-by-doctrine intellectual traversal

## 3. Intellectual referential stratification

Do not collapse founder, school, followers, and later heresiological category.

Define:

I0 — NAMED_FOUNDER
I1 — TEACHER / CLAIMED_AUTHORITY
I2 — IMMEDIATE_SCHOOL
I3 — LATER_FOLLOWERS / BRANCH
I4 — HERESIOLOGICAL_CATEGORY
I5 — CLAIMED_ANCESTRAL_SOURCE

Examples:

```text
Valentinus I0
→ immediate students I2
→ Ptolemy / Heracleon / Marcus branches I3
→ "Valentinians" I4
→ alleged Platonic/Pythagorean source I5
```

```text
Basilides I0
→ Isidore / immediate circle I2
→ later Basilidians I3/I4
```

A doctrine attested among later followers must not automatically be projected back to the founder.

## 4. Report strata

For every passage classify voice:

V0 — DIRECT_QUOTATION_OR_POEM
V1 — CLOSE_SOURCE_SUMMARY
V2 — AUTHOR_PARAPHRASE
V3 — AUTHOR_COMPARISON
V4 — AUTHOR_DERIVATION_CLAIM
V5 — POLEMICAL_CLASSIFICATION
V6 — LATER_TRANSLATION_OR_EDITORIAL_LAYER

The most important rule:

```text
V0/V1 evidence must not be silently merged with V4/V5 interpretation.
```

## 5. The Naassene node — first high-value linguistic packet

The Naassene report is especially valuable because it combines multiple languages and traditions inside one reported system.

### Name transform

The author connects **Naasseni** with a Hebrew word for serpent, represented in Greek as **Naas**.

Modern linguistic comparison should freeze the forms separately:

```text
Hebrew nāḥāš / נחש "serpent"
→ Greek-mediated Naas
→ ethnonym Naasseni
```

The report then links **Naas** with Greek **naos** "temple."

This should be tagged as:

```text
CROSS_LANGUAGE_FOLK_ETYMOLOGY / PHONO-SEMANTIC_REANALYSIS
```

not as historical Greek etymology.

This is a canonical contact/reanalysis example:
foreign carrier
→ Greek phonological approximation
→ attraction to a familiar Greek word
→ theological reinterpretation.

### Group-name stratification

According to the report:
- the group is called Naasseni,
- they also call themselves "Gnostics."

Do not assume either label has the same social scope.

Record:
- external/reporting label,
- claimed self-designation,
- internal subgroup if any,
- source witness.

### Mixed source field

The Naassene report combines or cites:
- Christian scripture,
- Homer,
- mystery traditions,
- Phrygian material,
- Egyptian material,
- Samothracian/Eleusinian traditions,
- a hymn preserved by the Refutation.

This makes it a miniature transposition nexus.

## 6. Naassene hymn as pace/source test

The preserved hymn should be isolated from the surrounding polemic.

For the Greek text:
- freeze critical edition,
- scan meter/cadence,
- segment cola,
- identify lexical repetitions,
- identify Christian versus philosophical/mystery vocabulary,
- compare quoted hymn structure with the author's prose summary.

Do **not** infer the Greek original's meter from an English verse translation.

Question:
Does the hymn preserve a native internal discourse pattern that disappears when the author paraphrases it?

## 7. Book IV — multilingual ritual register

The Refutation describes magical utterances as partly Greek and partly "as if" Hebrew, and refers to Hebrew characters/names.

This is high-value but low-trust linguistic evidence.

Record separately:
- actual recoverable Greek,
- alleged Hebrew,
- pseudo-Hebrew or unintelligible voces magicae,
- script description,
- author's language competence,
- ritual function.

The phrase "as if Hebrew" is itself evidence that the reporter perceived a foreign ritual register, not proof that the material is valid Hebrew.

## 8. Doxographic relay

Book I and later books preserve material from earlier philosophers, including important fragments of Presocratics.

Use:

```text
original philosopher
→ intermediary doxography/source
→ Refutation quotation/paraphrase
→ modern reconstruction
```

For each fragment:
- exact Greek wording,
- whether marked as quotation,
- parallel in Aristotle/Theophrastus tradition/Diogenes/Plutarch/etc.,
- doctrinal context in Refutation,
- whether the author reshapes it to support a heresy genealogy.

This is a direct analogue to later complete translations preserving lost older source material.

## 9. The author's genealogy-of-error operator

The work repeatedly uses a template like:

```text
HERESY H
is not genuinely new / scriptural
but derives from
PHILOSOPHER / ASTROLOGY / MAGIC / MYSTERY SOURCE S
```

This repeated argumentative cadence must itself be analyzed.

Do not promote:

```text
author claims H derives from S
```

into:

```text
historically demonstrated S → H
```

without independent evidence.

The derivation claim is V4.

It becomes a historical edge only if supported by:
- terminology,
- chronological possibility,
- contact,
- textual dependence,
- independent witnesses,
- shared unusual structure.

## 10. "Same doctrine, different phraseology"

Book VI explicitly frames some Valentinian teachers as advancing philosophical doctrines under altered wording.

This is directly relevant to the Transposition Engine.

Test:

```text
semantic/operator structure
vs.
technical lexicon
vs.
school-specific vocabulary
```

If a claimed parallel is real, we should be able to specify the transform rather than merely accept the author's assertion.

## 11. Founder → movement compression

Use the Tigranes/Armenians lesson.

Heresiological prose can move:

```text
named teacher
→ immediate pupils
→ school
→ later adherents
→ entire doctrinal category
```

without announcing every scope shift.

Examples to test:
- Simon → Simonists
- Valentinus → Valentinians
- Basilides → Basilidians
- Noetus → Noetians
- Elchasai → Elchasaites

This is **intellectual representative compression**.

It is especially dangerous when a later group doctrine is retrospectively attributed to a founder.

## 12. Anchor corpora

### Simon packet
Compare:
- Acts 8
- Justin Martyr
- Irenaeus
- Refutation
- later heresiologists

Measure:
historical person → legendary expansion → sect genealogy.

### Basilides packet
Compare:
- Clement of Alexandria
- Irenaeus
- Refutation
- later fragments

Modern scholarship notes substantial disagreement about which reported fragments represent Basilides himself versus later Basilidians.

### Valentinian packet
Compare:
- Irenaeus
- Clement
- Refutation
- Valentinian/Nag Hammadi materials where classification is secure

Keep:
Valentinus
≠ every Valentinian teacher
≠ every text later classified as Valentinian.

### Sethian packet
Compare:
- Refutation
- independent texts commonly classified as Sethian
- other heresiological reports

Use similarities to test the reporter, not to force every Sethian text into one church-like sect.

### Marcion packet
Compare:
- Refutation
- Tertullian
- Epiphanius
- reconstructed Marcionite Gospel/Apostolikon evidence

This is an ideal test for how hostile witnesses preserve and reshape an opponent's textual system.

### Elchasai packet
Compare:
- Refutation
- Origen/Eusebius reports
- Epiphanius
- Jewish-Christian and Aramaic linguistic context

Useful for contact between Semitic and Greek religious vocabulary.

## 13. Philosophumena ↔ Cyropaedia

Shared method:
both serialize heterogeneous populations around an authorial center.

But their operators are almost inverse.

Cyropaedia:
```text
difference
→ recognition
→ incorporation
→ coalition
```

Refutation:
```text
difference
→ classification
→ genealogy of error
→ exclusion
```

This is an analytical contrast, not a claim of literary relationship.

It helps reveal that the Refutation's taxonomy is part of its argument.

## 14. Philosophumena ↔ Gilgamesh lesson

Gilgamesh taught:
a later integrated work can preserve older material while reorganizing it.

Apply here:
a hostile third-century compiler can preserve genuine second-century quotations or technical vocabulary while embedding them in a misleading genealogy.

Therefore:

```text
polemical frame can be weak
while embedded lexical fragment is strong
```

The evidence confidence must be local, not assigned to the whole report.

## 15. Philosophumena ↔ biblical textual plurality lesson

If Irenaeus, Clement, Refutation, and Nag Hammadi witnesses differ, possible causes include:

1. reporter distortion,
2. different source documents,
3. chronological development within the movement,
4. different branches of a school,
5. different terminology for similar structures,
6. later editorial interpolation,
7. genuinely incompatible doctrines.

Do not assume "contradiction" until these possibilities are typed.

## Promotion rule

A Philosophumena relation receives two scores:

```text
C_preservation
C_genealogy
```

A passage may score high for preserving an ancient phrase and low for the author's claim about where that phrase came from.

This separation is mandatory.
