## Macro result

The `ATLANT-` carrier predates Plato's island narrative and is already distributed across cosmology, mountain geography, ethnonymy, and ocean naming.

```
ATLAS — cosmic bearer / pillar-holder
-> ATLAS — western Libyan mountain, "pillar of heaven"
-> ATLANTES — people explicitly named from the mountain
-> ATLANTIS [THALASSA] — the sea beyond the Pillars of Heracles
-> ATLANTIC western maritime zone in Herodotus
-> ATLANTIS [NESOS] — Plato's island
```

The strongest linguistic observation is that **Atlantis is not intrinsically an island-name**. In Herodotus it modifies/qualifies a sea; in Plato the same name-field modifies an island.

## 1. Homer — Atlas + sea knowledge + cosmic pillars

Odyssey 1.52-54 describes Atlas as knowing the depths of every sea and holding the tall pillars that keep earth and heaven apart.

```
ATLAS
+ SEA DEPTHS
+ COSMIC PILLARS
```

No North-African mountain localization is explicit here.

## 2. Hesiod — Atlas at the western edge

Theogony 517-520 places Atlas at the borders of the earth before the Hesperides, upholding the wide heaven.

This adds:

```
ATLAS
+ WESTERN EDGE
+ HEAVEN-SUPPORT
```

before Herodotus.

## 3. Herodotus 1.203 — Atlantis is a sea-classifier

Greek:

`ἡ ἔξω στηλέων θάλασσα ἡ Ἀτλαντὶς καλεομένη`

The sea beyond the Pillars is "called Atlantis." Herodotus then says that this sea, the sea navigated by Greeks, and the Erythraean Sea constitute one connected sea, while the Caspian is separate.

Thus:

```
ATLANTIS + THALASSA
= ATLANTEAN / ATLANTIC SEA
```

This is earlier than Plato's Atlantis island.

## 4. Herodotus 4.184 — mountain -> people

Herodotus names a western Libyan mountain `Atlas`. He reports that local inhabitants call it **the pillar of heaven** and explicitly says the neighboring people are **Atlantes because they took their name from the mountain**.

```
MOUNTAIN ATLAS
-> eponymy
-> PEOPLE ATLANTES
```

This is one of the cleanest ancient examples in the project of:

```
TOPONYM / LANDMARK
-> ETHNONYM
```

The "pillar of heaven" report also materially echoes the older Homeric/Hesiodic Atlas function, although Herodotus does not explicitly identify the local mountain with the Titan in this passage.

## 5. Herodotus 4.185 — Atlantes -> Pillars corridor

Immediately after the Atlantes notice, Herodotus says the ridge on which these peoples live extends as far as the Pillars of Heracles and beyond.

This gives:

```
ATLAS / ATLANTES ZONE
-> WESTWARD RIDGE
-> PILLARS OF HERACLES
-> BEYOND
```

The exact historical geography of the inland itinerary remains uncertain, but the textual topology is explicit.

## 6. The Pillars are a boundary-interface, not a terminal wall

Several Herodotean passages make the region beyond the Pillars operational:

- 4.8: Erytheia lies near Gadeira, outside the Pillars, on Ocean.
- 4.42: Necho's Phoenician sailors circumnavigate Libya and return through the Pillars.
- 4.43: Sataspes sails beyond the Pillars and south along the Libyan coast.
- 4.196: Carthaginians conduct silent trade with peoples beyond the Pillars.

Thus:

```
PILLARS
!= END OF WORLD
```

Instead:

```
PILLARS
= STRAIT / THRESHOLD
between Mediterranean and Atlantis/Atlantic maritime space
```

## 7. Plato changes the classifier, not the carrier

Timaeus 24e first says the invading power came from the `Ἀτλαντικὸν πέλαγος`, the Atlantic sea, and locates the relevant island in front of the mouth called the Pillars of Heracles.

At 25a Plato writes:

`ἐν δὲ δὴ τῇ Ἀτλαντίδι νήσῳ ταύτῃ`

"in this Atlantean/Atlantis island."

Compare directly:

```
HERODOTUS:
Ἀτλαντὶς [θάλασσα]
ATLANTIS + SEA

PLATO:
Ἀτλαντίς [νῆσος]
ATLANTIS + ISLAND
```

This is a canonical classifier-rebinding case.

```

```

## 8. Morphological carrier

Greek preserves one `ATLANT-` stem across the family:

```
Ἄτλας        Atlas
Ἄτλαντος     of Atlas
Ἄτλαντες     Atlantes
Ἀτλαντίς     Atlantean / Atlantis (feminine form)
Ἀτλαντικός   Atlantic / of Atlas
```

Therefore similarity among Atlas / Atlantes / Atlantis is not merely phonetic resemblance; it is a transparent Greek morphological family.

## 10. Productive subproblem — Atarantes vs Atlantes

Modern critical texts distinguish Herodotus' `Atarantes` from the later `Atlantes`, but the manuscript tradition is complicated: older scholarship reports that manuscripts often read `Atlantes` at the first occurrence, with `Atarantes` restored to distinguish the two populations.

This makes the pair useful as a textual-transmission control:

```
ATARANTES / ATLANTES
= manuscript-confusion-sensitive ethnonym pair
```

Do not use their similarity as historical kinship evidence until the textual apparatus is source-locked.

```

```

## 12. Hellanicus — a pre-Platonic *Atlantis / Atlantias* that is genealogical

Hellanicus of Lesbos (5th c. BCE, before Plato) wrote one of his major genealogical/mythographic works under the title **Atlantis / Atlantias**. Modern reference works place it beside his `Phoronis`, `Deukalioneia`, and `Asopis` as lineage-organizing monographs.

LSJ is explicit that feminine `Ἀτλαντίς` functions patronymically ('of Atlas') and is also the title of Hellanicus' work; `Ἀτλαντιάς` is an attested alternate title form.

Therefore the pre-Platonic name field now has an additional object class:

```text
ἈΤΛΑΝΤΙΣ / ἈΤΛΑΝΤΙΑΣ
= GENEALOGICAL WORK / ATLAS-LINEAGE CLASSIFIER
```

not merely sea or later island.

## 15. Euripides (428 BCE) independently fixes Atlas to the western maritime boundary

`Hippolytus` predates Plato and repeatedly uses Atlas as the far western boundary marker.

At the opening, Aphrodite's sphere covers those living between the sea/Euxine side and the **bounds of Atlas**.

At lines 742-747, the chorus imagines the apple-bearing shore of the Hesperides, where the ruler of the sea no longer grants sailors a path farther and where the sacred limit of the sky is held by Atlas.

At line 1053 Euripides uses `τερμόνων ... Ἀτλαντικῶν` ('Atlantean/Atlas bounds'); LSJ identifies these with the Pillars of Heracles.

Thus before Plato we have:

```text
HESPERIDES
+ NAVIGATIONAL LIMIT
+ ATLAS
+ ATLANTEAN BOUNDS
+ PILLARS-OF-HERACLES GEOGRAPHIC FUNCTION
```


## 16. Carrier inventory before Plato

By the late fifth century BCE, before Plato's island narrative, the `ATLANT-` family is already carrying at least five distinct classifiers:

```text
1. COSMIC PERSON      -> Atlas
2. MOUNTAIN           -> Atlas
3. PEOPLE             -> Atlantes
4. SEA / OCEAN        -> Atlantis thalassa
5. GENEALOGY / WORK   -> Atlantis / Atlantias
6. BOUNDARY ADJECTIVE -> Atlantean bounds
```

Plato adds:

```text
7. ISLAND             -> Atlantis nesos
```

The stronger historical formulation is therefore:

```text
PLATO DOES NOT CREATE THE ATLANT- CARRIER.
PLATO REBINDS AN OLD, MULTI-CLASSIFIER CARRIER TO A POLITICAL ISLAND.
```
