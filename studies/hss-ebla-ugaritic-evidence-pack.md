## 2. Ebla orthography explains the apparent z/s discrepancy

The Eblaite spelling `ḫa-zi-zu-um` cannot be evaluated by reading Latin transliteration letters as if
they represented a modern one-to-one alphabet.

Comparative Semitic work on the Ebla syllabary notes that the ZV sign series can represent several
Proto-Semitic sibilants, including *s and *z.

A specialist Semitic etymological discussion explicitly gives:

```text
geštu₃ = ḫa-zi-zu-um "ear" (VE 389)
~ Akkadian ḫasīsu
```

and also compares Arabic material.

Relevant source trail:
- Krebernik, "Zu Syllabar und Orthographie der lexikalischen Texte aus Ebla" (ZA 73, 1983)
- Semitic Etymological Dictionary discussion citing Krebernik and SED I no. 115
- Heidelberg record:
  https://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeumdok/1126/
- searchable reproduction of the SED discussion:
  https://dokumen.pub/roots-beginning-with-p-and-b-volume-1-1614513058-9781614513056-w-1257972.html

Operational consequence:

```text
Ebla ZV spelling
!= automatic /z/ phoneme
```

Therefore:

```text
ḫa-zi-zu-um
↔
ḫasīsu
```

does **not** require arbitrary z → s substitution invented by the Nexus.
The spelling system itself licenses a broader sibilant readout.

This materially strengthens HSS02.

## 4. Ugaritic ḫss is now supported by grammar/text scholarship rather than a generic secondary summary

Ugaritic texts write the craftsman deity as:

```text
kṯr w ḫss
```

A scholarly Ugaritic grammar vocalizes the second element:

```text
ḫss [ḫasisu / ḫasīsu]
= "wise, intelligent"
```

and identifies it as part of the craftsman god's full name.

Source:

Daniel Sivan, *A Grammar of the Ugaritic Language*:
https://e-learning.tsu.ge/pluginfile.php/5816/mod_resource/content/0/Sivan_AGrammarOfTheUgariticLanguage.pdf

The direct text locus used in the grammar includes the form `ḥss/ḫss` in KTU material.

Additional high-quality control:

ISAC / Oriental Institute, *"Like ʾIlu Are You Wise"*:
https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Publications/SAOC/saoc73.pdf

This work states that `ktr-w-hss` literally means "Skillful and Wise."

An older scholarly comparison is even more explicit:
Lichtenstein, JANES 4 (1972), notes that the conventional interpretation of the Ugaritic divine name
is demonstrated on the basis of Akkadian `ḥasīsu` "understanding":

https://janes.scholasticahq.com/api/v1/articles/2208-psalm-68-7-revisited.pdf

Therefore HSS03 is no longer supported merely by a generic secondary gloss.
