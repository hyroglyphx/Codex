# Homeric Transmission and Translation Research State

Status: ACTIVE — D4 TARGET
Updated: 2026-09-21

## Why Homer is unusually useful

The Iliad and Odyssey should not be modeled as single immutable texts. For Nexus purposes they form a layered transmission system:

Mycenaean / Arcado-Cypriot residues
→ Aeolic + Ionic poetic diction
→ oral/performance multiforms
→ early written/papyrus witnesses
→ Alexandrian editorial criticism
→ medieval Greek manuscript tradition
→ Latin and later translations/adaptations

Each arrow is a different operation.

## Primary Greek comparison lanes

### 1. Homeric Kunstsprache
Homeric Greek is a composite poetic language, predominantly Ionic with Aeolic and older/residual forms. The key research object is not merely dialect identification but which formulae preserve which linguistic layers.

### 2. Hellenistic papyri
Early papyri preserve textual variants and in some cases forms that differ from the later medieval standard. Treat these as multiform witnesses, not corruption by default.

### 3. Alexandrian editions
Zenodotus, Aristophanes of Byzantium, and Aristarchus represent editorial states. Later scholia preserve reports of readings, athetized lines, variant texts, and commentary from this scholarship.

### 4. Venetus A
The 10th-century Venetus A is the oldest complete Iliad manuscript and preserves scholia deriving from ancient Alexandrian/Roman Homeric scholarship. It is a late manuscript carrying much older variant information.

## Parallel Greek narrative corpora

The Epic Cycle provides adjacent/alternative Trojan material:
- Cypria
- Aethiopis
- Little Iliad
- Iliou Persis
- Nostoi
- Telegony

These are not translations of Homer. They are parallel Greek epic traditions covering material before, during, and after the Iliad/Odyssey and sometimes preserving alternative narrative sequences.

## Early cross-language translations/adaptations

### Livius Andronicus — Odusia
Third-century BCE Latin translation/adaptation of the Odyssey. This is one of the earliest and most important Greek→Latin epic transposition witnesses.

Questions:
- divine-name substitution,
- Greek hexameter → Latin Saturnian meter,
- syntax compression/expansion,
- Greek formula → Roman equivalent,
- cultural domestication.

### Ilias Latina
Probably Neronian-era Latin hexameter epitome of the Iliad, reducing the poem drastically. This is a compression benchmark rather than a literal translation benchmark.

## Bronze Age external comparison

Hittite records provide:
- Wilusa ~ Ilion/Ilios comparison,
- Ahhiyawa ~ Achaean/Mycenaean comparison,
- Alaksandu ~ Alexandros comparison.

These should be held at historical/onomastic-matrix status rather than treated as proof of direct Homeric textual continuity.

## New benchmark — HOM-01

Align one passage through:
1. early papyrus witness where available,
2. Alexandrian variant/scholion,
3. Venetus A,
4. modern critical Greek text,
5. Livius Andronicus or Ilias Latina where corresponding material exists,
6. Epic Cycle parallel if relevant.

Record:
- exact Greek,
- dialectal layer,
- formulaic morphology,
- variant words/lines,
- editorial judgment,
- translation/adaptation,
- preserved operator,
- omitted/added material,
- meter change,
- name/title transposition,
- confidence.

## High-value lexical experiments

Track words/titles with Mycenaean and later Greek depth:
- wanax / anax
- basileus
- potnia
- laos
- demos
- oikos
- kleos
- nostos
- xenos
- geras
- time

The point is to distinguish:
Mycenaean institutional meaning
→ Homeric poetic meaning
→ Classical Greek meaning
→ Latin translation choice.
