# Gilgamesh Multilingual Nexus Study

Status: ACTIVE — D5 TARGET
Updated: 2026-09-21

## Core principle

"The Epic of Gilgamesh" is a transmission ecology, not one fixed text.

The useful hierarchy is:

```text
Sumerian Gilgamesh/Bilgames compositions
    ↓ recomposition / adaptation
Old Babylonian Akkadian Gilgamesh
    ↓ regional circulation / recomposition
Middle Babylonian and peripheral Akkadian witnesses
    ├── Hattusa Akkadian
    ├── Ugarit Akkadian
    ├── Emar Akkadian
    └── Megiddo Akkadian
    ↓
Hittite adaptation + Hurrian renderings at Hattusa
    ↓
Standard Babylonian recension
    ↓
later Babylonian / Assyrian scholarly custody
```

The flood tradition intersects this graph from an older/parallel narrative family:

```text
Sumerian Flood Story: Ziusudra
Akkadian Atrahasis
        ↓
Utnapishtim flood episode in Gilgamesh XI
        ↓
Hebrew Genesis flood comparison
        ↓
Greek Berossus: Xisouthros
```

These are not all the same relation type.

## A. Sumerian compositions

Core texts:
- Gilgamesh and Akka
- Gilgamesh and Huwawa A
- Gilgamesh and Huwawa B
- Gilgamesh and the Bull of Heaven
- Gilgamesh, Enkidu and the Netherworld
- Death of Gilgamesh

These are independent compositions rather than tablets of a single Sumerian epic.

### High-value name states
- Sumerian Ḫuwawa → Akkadian Humbaba
- Sumerian Enkidu remains Enkidu in Akkadian tradition
- Sumerian Gilgamesh/Bilgames → Akkadian Gilgameš

Do not infer phonological history from conventional modern spellings alone; compare cuneiform spellings/manuscripts.

## B. Akkadian recomposition

Old Babylonian Akkadian creates a more continuous heroic composition from inherited Gilgamesh material.

Standard Babylonian later produces the canonical eleven-tablet form plus Tablet XII, with Tablet XI incorporating the flood narrative.

Bridge type:
RECENSION_RECOMPOSITION, not simple translation.

## C. Peripheral Akkadian witnesses

Late Bronze Age Akkadian Gilgamesh fragments are known from:
- Hattusa / Boğazköy
- Ugarit
- Emar
- Megiddo

This is crucial evidence that Akkadian literary Gilgamesh circulated far outside Babylonia.

These are same-language scribal transport witnesses and should be tested for:
- local orthography,
- local Akkadian grammar,
- lexical substitutions,
- tablet organization,
- omissions/additions,
- scribal-school conventions.

Megiddo is especially valuable because it places an Akkadian Gilgamesh witness in the southern Levant.

## D. Hattusa multilingual nexus

Hattusa preserves:
- two Akkadian Gilgamesh versions,
- fragmentary Hurrian renderings,
- a substantial Hittite-language Gilgamesh adaptation.

This is one of the project's highest-value ancient translation laboratories.

### Research questions
For aligned episodes:
1. what Akkadian proposition is retained?
2. what Hittite syntax replaces Akkadian syntax?
3. which divine/geographic terms are localized?
4. which episodes expand or contract?
5. what formulaic pacing becomes natural Hittite prose/poetry?
6. do Hurrian fragments agree more closely with Akkadian or Hittite states?

The Hittite text can preserve material missing from Mesopotamian witnesses, so target-language adaptation may become a control for reconstructing the source tradition.

## E. Hurrian Gilgamesh

Hurrian fragments at Hattusa include more than one composition/tradition and are too fragmentary for a simple full translation alignment.

Use as:
- multilingual evidence,
- name/form transposition,
- narrative episode control,
- Hurrian literary adaptation.

Do not encode "Akkadian → Hurrian → Hittite" as a fixed chain unless an episode specifically warrants it.

## F. Flood-name orbit

### Sumerian
Ziusudra

### Akkadian
Utnapishtim / Uta-napišti

A strong scholarly line treats Utnapishtim as an Akkadian rendering/equivalent of the Sumerian Ziusudra tradition.

### Atrahasis
Atrahasis is the hero of a related Akkadian flood tradition and is not merely another spelling of Utnapishtim.

### Greek
Berossus' Xisouthros / Xisuthros preserves a Greek form of the Babylonian/Sumerian flood-hero tradition.

### Hebrew
Noah is a distinct Hebrew name and literary figure, not a phonetic translation of Ziusudra/Utnapishtim.

Therefore separate:
```text
name equivalence
role equivalence
narrative inheritance
operator equivalence
```

## H. Native-form accommodation

Do not expect a Hittite, Hurrian, Hebrew, or Greek reception to preserve Akkadian cadence.

Measure separately:

```text
A = narrative architecture
D = discourse/formula structure
P = native prosodic realization
```

A target may preserve A strongly while replacing D and P.

For Babylonian Gilgamesh itself, the recovered corpus already treats its principal cadence as semantic parallelism, syntactic balance, repetition, and controlled variation rather than Greek-style fixed quantitative meter.

## I. Greek and Homeric comparison

Homeric parallels should be entered as structural or cultural-matrix comparisons, not translations.

High-value examples:
- Ishtar / Aphrodite divine complaint scenes
- heroic fame versus mortality
- companion loss and lament
- divine council
- journey across boundary zones
- monster/guardian encounters

Recent scholarship explicitly debates whether individual Homeric parallels require direct Gilgamesh reception or reflect a broader ancient Near Eastern/Greek stream of narrative tradition.

## J. Negative controls

### Elamite
Do not list an Elamite Gilgamesh translation. A tablet once interpreted as such was later recognized as an unrelated private letter.

### Ugaritic
Akkadian Gilgamesh material at Ugarit does not equal a Ugaritic-language translation unless an actual Ugaritic rendering is identified.

### Megiddo
The Gilgamesh fragment is Akkadian, not Hebrew/Canaanite.

These distinctions are essential.

## Highest-value linguistic experiment

Hattusa should be treated as the central Gilgamesh nexus:

```text
            Akkadian Gilgamesh
              /           \
        Hurrian         Hittite
              \           /
               Hattusa
```

Unlike remote motif comparison, this gives us the same heroic tradition inside one multilingual scribal environment.

That makes Gilgamesh an ideal ancient precursor to the later Hebrew/Greek/Aramaic/Latin scriptural relay.


## K. Beyond-cuneiform identity network

New calibration from:
- Qumran *Book of Giants*,
- Aelian, *De natura animalium* 12.21.

These witnesses require the Gilgamesh graph to extend beyond textual recension.

Earlier model:

```text
Sumerian compositions
→ Akkadian recomposition
→ peripheral Akkadian/Hurrian/Hittite witnesses
→ Standard Babylonian scholarly custody
```

Extended model:

```text
Mesopotamian Gilgamesh traditions
        │
        ├── cuneiform recensional/scribal line
        │
        ├── Levantine / Aramaic / giant-tradition reception
        │       └── Qumran Book of Giants
        │
        └── Greco-Roman reception
                └── Aelian Gilgamos
```

The later branches are not encoded as direct descendants of the Standard Babylonian recension unless specific evidence warrants that edge.

### New identity tensor

For every Gilgamesh witness record:

```text
I = (
  name_form,
  entity_identity,
  kingship_role,
  strength_role,
  dream_role,
  ontological_classifier,
  moral_classifier,
  episode_set,
  companion_network,
  receiving_tradition
)
```

### Qumran

Preserves:
- Gilgamesh identity carrier,
- giant/primordial classifier,
- dream/vision network,
- strength/judgment field,
- possible Humbaba-like relational residue.

### Aelian

Preserves:
- Gilgamos name,
- Babylonian kingship,
- royal destiny.

Rebinds:
- threatened succession,
- confined mother,
- infant exposure from height,
- eagle rescue,
- gardener upbringing.

### New rule

```text
name continuity
!=
plot continuity
!=
classifier continuity
```

and:

```text
relational network survival
>
isolated name survival
```

This extension should be used when testing whether later receptions preserve a text, a character, an episode, or only a smaller identity/operator bundle.

### Sargon/Moses/Gilgamos foundling comparison

```text

→ adoptive/gardener upbringing
```
