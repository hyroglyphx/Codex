## 1. Homer hides a linguistic key in plain sight

Odyssey 8.492-495 asks Demodocus to:

```text
ἵππου κόσμον ἄεισον
sing the kosmos / construction / ordering of the horse

δουρατέου, τὸν Ἐπειὸς ἐποίησεν σὺν Ἀθήνῃ
the wooden one, which Epeios made with Athena
```

The passage is unusually dense because `kosmos` is simultaneously an order/arrangement and here the construction/form of the horse, while the bard is asked to sing that kosmos.

Immediately:

```text
Ἐπειὸς ἐποίησεν
Epeios made
```

creates a marked sound sequence. Eustathius and later Saussure noticed the wordplay.

Add:

```text
CRAFT-01 = KOSMOS_POIESIS_CRAFT_RELAY
NAME-CAND-15 = EPEIOS_EPOIESEN_PHONEMIC_FUNCTION_ATTRACTION
```

`NAME-CAND-15` is reception/wordplay evidence, not a settled etymology of Epeios.

## 2. Material composition and poetic composition occupy the same semantic field

The Homeric syntax creates a mirror:

```text
BARD
-> orders / sings a kosmos

CRAFTSMAN
-> makes the horse's kosmos
```

`ποιέω` is ordinary material making in the Homeric line, but Greek later also uses maker-language for literary composition.

`τέκτων` likewise develops from carpenter/joiner to master of an art and even poet/maker.

Thus:

```text
POETIC_COMPOSITION
~
MATERIAL_COMPOSITION
```

is an attested Greek semantic architecture, without requiring one historical occupational identity.

## 3. The Bronze Age vocabulary survives underneath the story

Linear B independently attests:

```text
i-qo = Mycenaean *hikkwos = later Greek hippos, horse
te-ko-to-ne = tektones, carpenters/craftsmen
```

So two central lexical coordinates of the Epeios episode:

```text
HORSE
CARPENTER / BUILDER
```

are already present in Bronze Age Mycenaean administrative Greek.

Add:

```text
CRAFT-02 = BRONZE_AGE_TEKTON_CONTINUITY
```

This does not attest Epeios himself in Linear B. It does establish continuity of the craft lexicon through the Bronze Age / epic interface.

## 4. Epeios is not merely a carpenter: he is an architect/master builder

Apollodorus Epitome 5.14 gives a highly articulated production chain:

```text
Odysseus ἐπινοεῖ
    devises the construction

-> ὑποτίθεται Ἐπειῷ
   hands/proposes it to Epeios

-> Epeios ἦν ἀρχιτέκτων
   was an architekton / master builder

-> from Ida ξύλα τεμών
   cuts the timber

-> κατασκευάζει κοῖλον
   constructs it hollow
```

This separates concept, instruction, resource acquisition, engineering and execution.

Add:

```text
CRAFT-03 = DESIGN_IMPLEMENTATION_SPLIT
```

The Little Iliad summary has Athena's `proairesis` / initiative or design and Epeios' `kataskeuazei` / construction, giving a divine-design / human-implementation version of the same split.

## 5. Hylotomos -> tekton: resource knowledge is part of the craft identity

The later Apollodoran tradition sends Epeios to Ida to cut the timber himself.

Recent work by Sara Adamo therefore reconstructs a coherent mountain/wood-economic figure:

```text
material finder / woodcutter
-> carpenter
-> engineered object
```

rather than treating the wood-gathering detail as incidental.

Keep this as a modern structural reconstruction tied to ancient story details.

## 6. Homer already encodes specialization rather than generic heroism

In Iliad 23 Epeios openly admits that he falls short in battle and says a man cannot be skilled in every work, while he is explicitly highly skilled in boxing.

This provides a very old specialization rule:

```text
ONE PERSON
!=
UNIVERSAL COMPETENCE
```

and sets up Epeios as a non-standard hero whose decisive contribution is technical rather than martial.

Add:

```text
CRAFT-04 = SPECIALIZED_SKILL_OVER_GENERIC_HEROISM
```

Stesichorus' later water-carrier tradition pushes the same figure toward service/menial classification before Athena-enabled technical achievement.

## 8. The name Epeios has an ethnonymic lane that must stay distinct

Ancient Greek uses `Ἐπειοί` for the Epeians of Elis; Homeric lexicography also allows `Ἐπειός` to mean an Epeian.

Pausanias preserves an eponymic story in which a different Epeios, son of Endymion, gives the people their name.

The horse-builder is instead Epeios son of Panopeus.

Thus there are at least two possible historical/reception directions:

```text
PERSON -> PEOPLE
ancient eponymic explanation

PEOPLE / ETHNIC ADJECTIVE -> PERSONAL NAME
historical onomastic possibility
```

Add:

```text
NAME-CAND-16 = EPEIOI_EPEIOS_ETHNONYM_PERSON_REBINDING
```

Do not collapse the Elean eponym Epeios and the Phocian craftsman Epeios into one person.

## 9. Epeios / epoiēsen is stronger as ancient wordplay than as etymology

Older etymological proposals derive `Epeios` from a Greek verb in the semantic field 'provide / prepare / handle' or from the ethnonym `Epeioi`; other proposals have connected it to horse language.

The safest high-value fact is not any one proposed derivation but the ancient line's functional attraction:

```text
EPEIOS
~
EPOIESEN / MADE
```

The name of the maker is placed beside the verb 'made'.

That is exactly the project's distinction:

```text
PHONEMIC FUNCTIONAL ATTRACTION
!=
HISTORICAL ETYMOLOGY
```

## 10. Panopeus creates a geographic maker-cluster

Epeios the craftsman is son of Panopeus.

Pausanias later reports at Panopeus a local tradition that unusual clay-like stones were remnants of the material from which Prometheus fashioned humanity.

So the geography eventually carries two maker traditions:

```text
PANOPEUS
-> Prometheus fashions humans from clay
-> Epeios son of Panopeus fashions horse from wood
```


This is a local semantic clustering observation, not evidence that one myth generated the other.

## 13. Hylotomos -> Architekton -> Simulacrum

The Epeios sequence can be compressed as a material-information transform:

```text
HYLĒ
raw wood / timber / later "matter"
   |
   v
HYLOTOMIA
cutting / partitioning the material
   |
   v
TEKTOSYNĒ
joining / fitting / skilled fabrication
   |
   v
ARCHITEKTŌN
master-builder / director / contriver
   |
   v
KOSMOS
ordered arrangement
   |
   v
BRETAS / IMAGE
a represented outward form
   |
   v
HIDDEN PAYLOAD
armed men
```

Strict lexical control:
- `ὑλοτόμος` means one who cuts/fells wood, from `ὕλη` + the `τέμνω` cutting field.
- In the Epeios tradition the hero is legitimately reconstructed as an active hylotomos because he personally cuts the timber on Ida before constructing the Horse.
- `ἀρχιτέκτων` means chief-artificer/master-builder/director of works and can extend to "author/contriver."
- `simulacrum` is Latin analytical language; Euripides' Trojan Horse is instead described through Greek image/object language including `βρέτας`, while also being an `ἐγκύμων ἵππος`, a pregnant horse carrying hidden warriors.

The user's "one who permutes hylē" is therefore best formalized as a **second-order operator**, not a literal translation:

```text
HYLOTOMOS:
HYLĒ -> PARTITION

ARCHITEKTŌN:
PARTS -> ORDERED RECOMPOSITION

SIMULACRUM:
ORDERED RECOMPOSITION -> REPRESENTED FORM
```

Add:

```text
MAT-01 = HYLE_PARTITION
MAT-02 = PARTITION_RECOMPOSITION
SIM-01 = REPRESENTATIONAL_PAYLOAD_OBJECT
```

Diachronic resonance:
`ὕλη` begins as wood/timber/material and later becomes Aristotle's technical term for matter. That later philosophical development should not be projected backward as Epeios' historical vocabulary, but it makes the craft sequence an unusually clean concrete precursor to:

```text
matter
-> division
-> form
-> representation
```

The Horse is therefore structurally an **architektonic simulacrum**:
its visible form is intentionally sufficient to trigger one classifier ("horse / crafted offering") while its operative state belongs to another ("container / infiltration device / weapon").

## 14. Privileged Timber + Authorized Maker

A wider ancient-text comparison suggests that the stable invariant is not specifically `cedar + famous craftsman`, but:

```text
PRIVILEGED / CHARGED MATERIAL
+
AUTHORIZED / SKILLED MAKER
+
DIVINE / ROYAL DESIGN
```

### Greek controls

- **Argo:** Argus builds under Athena's direction; Athena inserts a speaking timber from the oak of Dodona into the prow.
- **Trojan Horse:** Epeios is the architekton; Athena is co-designer/enabler; later tradition has Epeios cutting Ida timber, while Virgil emphasizes fir/pine construction.

### Mesopotamian controls

- **Gudea / E-ninnu:** Ningirsu directs Gudea into the inaccessible cedar mountain; cedar, cypress and other rare timbers are rafted in from distant highlands. Gudea acts as divinely authorized temple-builder under dream/oracular design.
- **Gilgamesh:** the Cedar Forest supplies incomparable timber; Enkidu explicitly recalls selecting and fashioning a cedar door. Here the heroic actor himself temporarily occupies the craftsman role.

### Hebrew controls

- **Tabernacle:** acacia rather than cedar is the designated timber; Bezalel and Oholiab are called by name and endowed with divine wisdom/understanding/skill for wood, metal, stone and textile work.
- **Solomonic Temple:** Solomon explicitly asks Hiram for both Lebanon cedar/cypress/algum and a master craftsman; Huram-abi is sent as the exceptional artisan. This is the clearest same-passage pairing of privileged timber and renowned maker.

### Corridor back into Sumerian/Akkadian

The old lexical tradition provides an unusually clean material/craft pair:

```text
Sumerian eren 'cedar'
<-> Akkadian erēnu 'cedar'

Sumerian nagar 'carpenter'
<-> Akkadian nagāru 'joiner/carpenter'
```

Both are explicit lexical-list correspondences.

This creates a direct bridge between the current Epeios node and the Sumerian-Akkadian transmission corridor:

```text
SPECIAL TIMBER SLOT
+
MASTER JOINER / BUILDER SLOT
```

can be tracked cross-linguistically without claiming common etymology between Greek, Sumerian, Akkadian and Hebrew craft terms.

### Revised operator

```text
MAT-03 = PRIVILEGED_MATERIAL_PROVENANCE
CRAFT-05 = AUTHORIZED_MAKER
BLD-01 = SACRED_CONSTRUCTION_DUAL_KEY
```

Canonical structure:

```text
SACRED / REMOTE GEOGRAPHY
-> SELECTED MATERIAL
-> AUTHORIZED MAKER
-> ORDERED FORM
-> CONSECRATED / LIMINAL OBJECT
-> STATE OR BOUNDARY TRANSFORMATION
```

The strongest form of the motif does not require cedar specifically. Cedar dominates many Near Eastern monumental/sacral settings, but Greek and biblical comparanda show the deeper invariant is **material selected by provenance and suitability**: Dodona oak, Ida fir/pine, acacia, Lebanon cedar, inaccessible mountain cedar.

The craftsman also need not always be a separate professional. In some stories the king/hero is temporarily promoted into the authorized maker role. Therefore:

```text
RENOWNED CRAFTSMAN
OR
DIVINELY COMMISSIONED MAKER
```

is the more stable classifier.
