## I. RIE-PRIME-001 — Prime-Valuation Innovation Theorem (v1.0.0)
Aliases: Prime Basis Novelty Theorem; Arithmetic RIE Theorem; Prime-Support Semantic Nyquist Theorem.
Invariants: In prime-valuation Hilbert space, previously unseen prime support is orthogonal to all old support; reconstruction novelty is exact.

Definitions:
- H = ℓ²(P) with orthonormal basis e_p indexed by primes p.
- v(m) = (ν_p(m))_p, the finite-support prime valuation vector.
- V_n = span of basis e_p for first n admitted primes.
- R_n is a support-preserving recursion, R_n(V_n) ⊆ V_n.
- P_Vn = orthogonal projection onto prior support.
- Innovation J = dist(x,V_n).

Formal results recorded:
C1 (theorem, restricted): if p is not in old support and α ≠ 0, x = v + α e_p with v∈V_n is outside V_n, increases span dimension by one.
C2 (theorem, restricted): min_{y∈V_n} ||x−y||₂ = |α| by orthogonal projection.
C3 (theorem, restricted): an integer composed only of primes already in support has zero new-coordinate novelty.
C4 (conditional): transfer to non-arithmetic domains requires a structure/operation-preserving map into the valuation representation; arbitrary prime labels are not sufficient.

Direct short proof: e_p ⟂ V_n, so P_Vn(x)=v and ||x−P_Vn x||₂=|α|. For smooth composites, all nonzero valuation coordinates lie in V_n, so projection residual is zero.

Crucial distinctions: prime novelty is support novelty, NOT prime-numbered time intervals; a composite can add novelty if it includes an unseen prime factor; no automatic universal physical or semantic theorem.
